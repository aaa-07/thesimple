01 / THE PLAYER DECISION

Before someone registers, they are already asking six questions.

Is this for my level? Will I get enough matches? What format is it? Who is it for? Is the entry worth it? What happens if I lose early? AERYX makes the tournament answer before the player has to ask.

DISCOVERY

Format becomes part of the product.

Pools → knockout, progressive placement, league-first, junior festival, open competition. The structure changes whether a player wants to enter.

TRUST

Transparent entry, draw and result context.

Verified tournament information, categories, capacity and competition structure reduce uncertainty before payment.

02 / ONE ROUTE THROUGH COMPETITION

Discover → Enter → Prepare → Compete → Rank → Return.

The product is designed so each stage gives the next stage more meaning.

DISCOVER

Find the right event

Level, category, format, venue, prize and event character.

ENTER

Register as a player

Identity, partner/pair, category and payment remain connected.

PREPARE

Know your route

Fixture, schedule, group and what happens next.

COMPETE

Stay oriented live

Court calls, scores, fixtures and progression.

RETURN

Carry the result forward

Rating, rank, history, coins and the next opportunity.

03 / EVENT DAY

The venue can be noisy. The interface should be calm.

On match-day the priority changes from discovery to orientation: where am I playing, when, against whom, what is live, what changed and where do I stand?

FIXTURES

Schedule without hunting.

Fixtures become a destination, not a forwarded image that goes stale.

LIVE

Scores and courts stay connected.

Live match, court calls, score state and event progression belong together.

RESULT

The event has memory.

Winners, scores and moments remain useful after the crowd leaves.

05 / ACADEMY COMPETITION

Weekly academy matches should not feel disposable.

The same competitive identity can extend into academy life: match rating, player pass, challenge participation and recurring internal competition can make development feel visible between open tournaments.

ACADEMY MATCH RATING

A Friday match can change the player’s story.

Internal competition gains consequence without pretending to be a professional ranking system.

PLAYER PASS

Identity travels with participation.

Entries, schedule, rewards and event history can belong to one player instead of resetting every tournament.

06 / RETURN ENGINE

The strongest tournament product starts working after the tournament.

Rating, rankings, coins, rewards, store value and history give players a reason to look forward rather than only look back.

RATING

Competition compounds.

Open-tournament and academy-match ratings make repeated participation meaningful.

REWARDS

Between-match value.

Coins and useful rewards let participation create value outside the court schedule.

STORE

Identity can become tangible.

Kits, passes and tournament editions become part of the ecosystem rather than a separate shop.

HISTORY

Players can remember the journey.

Results and events become a competitive archive instead of disappearing posts.

09 / WHY WE BUILT IT THIS WAY

A bracket ends. A competitive relationship should not.

The design principle came from looking at the full emotional arc: anticipation before entry, uncertainty on event day, consequence after a match and the desire to know what comes next.

BEFORE

Reduce uncertainty

Make the event understandable enough to choose.

DURING

Protect attention

Players should think about badminton, not hunt for information.

AFTER

Preserve consequence

A result should add to an identity.

BETWEEN

Create useful return

Ratings, rewards and academy competition keep momentum alive.

NEXT

Make discovery smarter

The next tournament has context because the previous one exists.
